# SIH — What Wins Here (2024–2025 read)

**Confidence: high on structure, medium on "why a specific team won."** Grounded in the official
2024/2025 grand-finale result tables (all winning PS IDs + statuses), the archived 2024 problem-statement
page with final submission counts, the 2026 SPOC guidelines PDF, and winner write-ups.

## The winning shape
SIH is not one contest. It is ~250 tiny contests, one per problem statement. The organization that owns
a PS picks **4–5 finalist teams** for it and crowns **1–2 winners** (₹1.5L each). In 2024, **245 distinct
PS produced a winner out of ~254 total — 96%.** Almost every PS that runs crowns someone.

Consequence: your competition is only the teams on *your* PS, and the finale is close to "win your PS or
go home." So the whole game is (1) get shortlisted, then (2) out-build 4 other teams. Both are won at PS
selection, not at the keyboard.

## Recurring judging signals (what shows up in winners)
- **A working prototype, not a concept.** Every winner write-up stresses a live, demoable build by the finale.
- **Deep problem understanding.** Repeated Q&A failure mode: teams that "didn't fully understand the PS."
  Winners read the full description and defend the root cause, not a generic solution.
- **Feasibility over flash.** Judges reward a well-scoped build the team clearly executed over an ambitious
  one they can't finish. "Simple + practical + innovative + impactful."
- **Clear metric / clear impact.** A number the judge remembers (accuracy %, time saved, cost cut).
- **PPT that follows the official template exactly.** Incomplete or off-format decks get cut at screening.

## Where winners cluster (2024 software, by theme)
Smart Automation (47), Miscellaneous (28), Smart Education (26), Blockchain & Cybersecurity (19),
Transportation & Logistics (10). These are big because the *field* is big there — not proof of easy odds.

## The saturated zones (avoid or twist hard)
- **Student Innovation** (open category): ~500 submissions each, every edition. Maximum crowd.
- **Consumer-friendly themes** — health, women's safety, tourism, farmer apps, smart-city traffic, virtual
  gardens, education: 300–500 submissions. Everyone's first instinct lands here.

## The open gaps (low crowd, strong odds for a capable team)
- **Defence / space / intel orgs** — NTRO, ISRO, DRDO, MoES, MoD: ~100–130 submissions vs 300+ elsewhere.
  The jargon and specialism scare off casual teams. A team that can actually build here faces ~⅓ the crowd.
- **"Boring-looking" infra/forensics/compliance PS** — log pipelines, config auditors, forensic tools:
  low crowd because they read as tedious, but very buildable by a strong systems team.

## Common tech stacks (feasibility read)
Full-stack web (React/Next + Python/Node) + a focused AI/ML model + a dashboard is the default winning
build. Offline/air-gapped requirements appear in NTRO/defence PS — plan for no-cloud demos there.
