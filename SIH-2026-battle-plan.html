The one number that moves your odds

In 2024, submissions per software PS ranged from 56 to 500. Same prize, same finale, same 4–5 shortlist slots. A team on a 56-crowd PS had roughly 9× the odds of a team on a 500-crowd PS. This is the whole game, and it is decided before you write a line of code.

The deck they actually score

The official 2026 template is byte-for-byte the same structure as 2025. It is rigid on purpose: 6 slides maximum including the title, fixed headings you fill but cannot rename, and you upload a PDF. No PPT, Word, or other format is accepted. Download it from sih.gov.in › Idea PPT.

01

Title

PS ID, title, theme, category, team ID & name. Get every field right.

02

Proposed Solution

The idea, how it solves the problem, and what makes it novel.

03

Technical Approach

Stack + methodology. This is where an architecture diagram earns marks.

04

Feasibility

Feasibility, the real risks, and how you beat them. Shows maturity.

05

Impact & Benefits

Who it helps and the social / economic gain. Put a number here.

06

Research & References

Citations and links. Proves you understood the problem's roots.

Community-reported split. The official guideline lists: novelty, complexity, clarity, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, scale of impact, UX, and future scope.

What past winners repeat
  • Show a working prototype, not a concept. Every winner had one at the finale.
  • Read the full PS and defend the root cause. The classic elimination is "you didn't understand the problem."
  • No essays on slides. Points, diagrams, infographics. Bold the keywords.
  • One number they remember — accuracy, time saved, cost cut.
  • Follow the template exactly. Off-format or incomplete decks get cut at screening.
07

The playbook

Submit to two problem statements, not one.

The rules let one team enter up to 2 PS. That's two independent shortlist lotteries for the same build effort. Use both.

Pair a demo-magnet with a low-crowd technical pick.

One PS where the live demo lands hard in the room, one where the crowd is thinnest. Below, that's SIH26155 and SIH26145 — and their tooling overlaps, so you prep once.

Watch the live counter and be ready to jump.

Each PS shows ideas-submitted live and freezes at 500. If your pick heats up, a near-identical NTRO cyber PS is one row away. Don't marry a PS before you submit.

Build the one screen the judge sees.

Pick the single "magic moment" per PS (upload a file → instant verdict) and make that flow real and beautiful. Fake the rest until the finale.

Over-prepare the Q&A on the problem, not the tech.

Deep-tech judges probe whether you understand their world. Learn the domain enough to defend the root cause. That's where first-timers lose the room.
